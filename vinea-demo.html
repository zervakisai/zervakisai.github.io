Should I water the vines? Can I spray?

Two questions a vineyard manager answers every morning, and both cost real money to get wrong. Vinea answers them overnight — reading each block's hourly weather so the advice is waiting before work starts.

What you are looking at

Two things stated plainly. This is a replay, not a live service — these pages are static files, and the system's database, queue and overnight worker are not running behind them. And every advisory here was produced without calling the AI, so the wording is templated rather than written. The physics is identical either way; what the AI adds is judgement on the close nights and language a grower can act on.

Check it yourself

Open “Check the arithmetic” on any night. Water used, minus rain that soaked in, capped at what the soil can hold. If the sum disagrees with the headline, the headline is wrong.

Missing stays missing

A gap in the weather data is counted and lowers the confidence — never quietly filled with a zero, which would make a dry week look wet.

Two vineyards, one codebase

Nemea and Naoussa differ because their weather differs, not because anything was hand-tuned for the demo.

Under the hood

A small graph of typed agents — irrigation, spray, coordinator — on top of a deterministic FAO-56 core they are held to by output validators. Around it: Postgres row-level security, so a forgotten filter returns nothing rather than everything; service objectives measured in SQL with an error budget written before the first breach; hashed, revocable API keys with an access log; a Helm chart whose migration must succeed before any new pod serves traffic; and self-hosted tracing. Twelve decision records say why — including the one that deletes a feature the measurements did not support.
